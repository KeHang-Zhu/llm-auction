# 1. ATTACK: why the S3+S1 fusion still looks stapled together

The fusion's central problem is not terminology; it is identification. “Recognition” is asked to explain every favorable behavioral response, every unfavorable response, and even behavior that looks correct for the wrong reason. The paper currently has no independent measure that distinguishes recognition from instruction-following, salience, retrieval, or merely producing the intended bid. Result D is devastating here: every lever that changes bids leaves stated reasoning unchanged, the safety invariant is echoed in 0/600 treated traces, verbalized understanding mediates at most 35% of the effect, and the lever that changes stated reasoning does not change bids. That establishes that traces are poor behavioral diagnostics. It does **not** establish a hidden, nonverbal faculty called recognition. If “recognition” is inferred whenever bids improve and “fake recognition” is inferred whenever correct bids later prove brittle, the construct is an unfalsifiable homunculus.

D therefore cuts against S3 (“Recognition, not computation”) at least as strongly as it supports it. The favorable reading is that recognition can operate without articulation. But that reading requires an independent intervention or outcome that identifies recognition rather than simply relabeling the bid response. The unfavorable reading is that the safety sentence acts as a directive or salient answer cue even though the model never represents why it is valid. F makes that alternative especially serious: false invariant sentences move incumbent bids in the direction of the lie, including 75–89% overbidding, even though the prompt contains the information needed to reject the lie. Because F is an unpublished pilot pending coauthor ratification, it cannot yet bear a central causal claim; if confirmed, it shows content-credulity, not recognition of a true invariant. Calling the response to the true sentence “recognition” and the parallel response to the false sentence “corruption of recognition” protects S3 from the most natural falsification.

The “recognition hierarchy” also bends B and C beyond what they identify. B establishes a highly useful **ranking of interventions** across four incumbent families—OSP clock (rho about .97), safety-invariant text (+.78), payoff-tree scaffold (+.59), clock framing (+.30 and sign-mixed), baseline/menu restatement, then harmful belief/lookahead and risk prompts—with substantial cross-family agreement (Kendall's W=.70, p=.004). C establishes an invariance-content pattern: explaining why truth-telling is safe helps, while restating mechanics is null to harmful, with human anchors agreeing on the observed rungs. Neither result isolates a common latent recognition channel. OSP structure changes the extensive-form decision problem; an invariant sentence changes semantic content; a payoff tree changes representation; and belief/lookahead prompts change the task the model appears to perform (B, C). **Assumption:** absent a common-factor experiment not described in the brief, their rank order is evidence about behavioral implementation, not a measurement scale for recognition.

A Management Science or GEB referee can then identify the staple line. The first paper is S3: a cognitive claim about what makes truth-telling obvious. The second is S1 (“LLM panel as ordinal design-screening tool”): a validation-and-cost claim about using model panels to rank treatments. A and G justify only the second, narrower claim. The models reproduce the human FPSB-versus-SPSB difficulty ordering and winner's-curse comparative statics while making opposite-signed baseline errors (A), and the 95-cell search fails to match human levels and directions across mechanisms (G). This supports an ordinal screen and rules out a cardinal human proxy; it does not validate “recognition” as the shared human–LLM mechanism. The winner's-curse evidence in A is worth keeping because the PI requires it and because it broadens ordinal validation, but winner's-curse attenuation is not itself evidence about recognizing truth-telling safety in a strategy-proof mechanism. Under the fusion, it is visibly an instrument-validation module attached before the cognitive paper begins.

The S1 payoff is also presently promised rather than demonstrated. H makes the potential value vivid—about $10 per treatment cell versus $2,000–$15,000 for human experiments—but low cost alone does not show that the screen improves a design decision. A validates rankings on already-known human patterns; C reports agreement with existing human anchors; the genuinely discriminating +X exercise and the combined-lever design map are still PLANNED. Until the paper makes and survives a held-out prediction, “screening instrument” is a prospective use case, not an established result. This is exactly S1's stated weakness: screening “begs for a demonstrated discovery.”

The frontier material does belong in **a** coherent paper, but not comfortably in S3 as currently formulated. E says frontier models are truthful at baseline, so the positive recognition hierarchy has no room to operate; a menu restatement then collapses one frontier model into flawlessly executed first-price logic, and affiliation wording triggers a misapplied winner's-curse script that IPV wording removes. F adds the opposing pattern that frontier models resist false invariant sentences yet ignore true rules under unfamiliar wording. Those facts support a population-specific taxonomy—incumbent under-computation/content-credulity versus frontier mis-retrieval/format-credulity (E, F)—rather than a single recognition scale. S3 saves the hierarchy by calling frontier success “faked recognition,” but that label is currently retrospective: baseline behavior alone supplies no test that separates genuine recognition from retrieval. The planned +X auctions are explicitly the missing discriminator because “bid your value” is wrong there. Until +X is run, the fusion is claiming the answer to the test it says is needed.

So the blunt referee verdict will be: the frontier section either fractures the recognition thesis or becomes a collection of fascinating LLM anecdotes; the ordinal-screen section either remains a modest validation result or needs a prospective success; and the word “recognition” papers over rather than resolves the gap. The evidence is stronger than that framing.

# 2. PROPOSE: behavioral implementation robustness

## (a) One-sentence story

Incentive compatibility is a property of a mechanism, but successful implementation with bounded and artificial participants is a joint property of the mechanism, its representation, and the reasoner; an ordinally human-validated LLM panel provides a cheap behavioral stress test that ranks structural and message designs and exposes population-specific failures under redescription, misleading content, and memorized-rule transfer (A–H, with F still pilot evidence and +X still PLANNED).

## (b) Six-step arc

1. **Start with the implementation gap, not a mental construct.** Strategy-proof rules can still produce nontruthful behavior, and economically equivalent or nearby presentations can change that behavior: incumbent baselines underbid, mechanics restatements are null to harmful, and some strategizing prompts backfire (A–C), while a frontier model moves from truthful bidding to severe underbidding after a menu restatement (E). These are observable failures of behavioral implementation; the paper need not decide whether an unobserved faculty recognized anything.

2. **The implementation gap forces a screening problem.** A designer needs to know which rule-and-message packages remain behaviorally sound for the intended participant population, but exhaustive human testing costs $2,000–$15,000 per experiment compared with roughly $10 per LLM treatment cell (H). That cost difference motivates an LLM screen, but does not validate one.

3. **The need for a screen forces a disciplined validation ceiling.** The panel reproduces the human ordering of FPSB versus SPSB difficulty and winner's-curse comparative statics even though humans and LLMs make opposite-signed baseline errors (A); moreover, the same design levers repair those opposite errors (A). The 95-cell failure to match human levels and directions makes the boundary equally important: the panel can rank vulnerabilities and interventions, not stand in for human bid levels (G). This houses the required winner's-curse result as evidence for ordinal sensitivity rather than as evidence for recognition.

4. **Once the instrument is justified, use it to build the intervention ranking.** Across four incumbent families, OSP clock structure dominates truthful safety-invariant text, which dominates payoff-tree and clock-framing scaffolds; mechanics restatement does not help on average, and belief/lookahead prompts backfire (B). C sharpens the actionable result: content explaining why truth-telling is safe helps, while additional mechanics do not, with every observed rung aligned with the cited human anchors. The economic object is a ranked menu of implementation designs, not a scale of latent cognition.

5. **A stress test must audit behavior under perturbation, because explanations cannot certify it.** D shows a complete dissociation between behavioral movement and stated plans: helpful levers do not produce articulable understanding, and improved articulation does not produce better bids. Therefore the screen should use revealed robustness—bid stability and direction under controlled changes in structure, wording, and prompts—rather than trace fluency as its endpoint. F, if confirmed, is the adversarial-content test: incumbents follow false invariants while frontier models resist them, so a baseline pass is insufficient.

6. **Population shifts then force transfer tests, which make the frontier results the culmination rather than a footnote.** E reveals two regimes: incumbent failures look like under-computation, whereas frontier failures arise when surface cues retrieve and execute the wrong playbook; F provisionally refines this into content- versus format-credulity. The PLANNED 2P+X/AC+X cells are the decisive transfer test because the familiar “bid your value” string is wrong, and the PLANNED lie-on-clock cell tests whether OSP structure remains robust to hostile message content. The final product is a population-specific stress-testing protocol: validate ordinally where human anchors exist, make explicit held-out predictions where they do not, and re-run the cheap screen when either the mechanism representation or participant technology changes (A, E–H).

## (c) First three sentences of the abstract

Strategy-proofness guarantees incentives, not that bounded or artificial participants will implement the intended strategy when the same economic logic is presented through different structures, messages, and surface cues (B–F). We develop a roughly $10-per-treatment-cell LLM panel as an ordinal stress test: it reproduces the human difficulty ordering and winner's-curse comparative statics despite opposite-signed baseline errors, while a 95-cell search rejects matching human bid levels and directions across mechanisms (A, G, H). The panel ranks OSP clock structure above truthful safety-invariant text and generic scaffolds, bounds mediation through verbalized understanding at 35%, and reveals a frontier regime in which baseline truthfulness can collapse under a menu restatement because a memorized but incorrect playbook is retrieved (B–E).

# 3. GAP LIST, ranked by necessity

1. **Run the human-anchored 2P+X/AC+X experiment as a prospective transfer test.** This is non-negotiable. E motivates the retrieval-selection account, but current frontier evidence identifies it from one menu collapse and one affiliation/IPV wording contrast. The PLANNED +X design is cleaner because it makes the memorized “bid your value” rule incorrect. Before running it, record model-family-specific predictions and the behavioral criterion for “unadjusted-value bidding”; then compare all incumbent and frontier families with Li's human anchors. No particular +X outcome is established in the brief, so the paper must not write the predicted frontier failure as a result. Without this experiment, “retrieval selection” remains a persuasive interpretation of E rather than a demonstrated transfer failure, and the required +X addition has no role in the paper's identification.

2. **Turn F from a pending pilot into a confirmatory, matched adversarial-content experiment.** Coauthor ratification is explicitly pending, yet the strongest evidence that true safety language may operate through credulity rather than understanding comes from F. The minimal confirmatory design needs matched true-invariant, false-invariant, and neutral text under the same auction rule, value support, and model families, with the direction and magnitude outcomes fixed in advance. Without that, the paper should report F as exploratory and cannot make content-credulity one of its two frontier/incumbent regimes.

3. **Pre-specify an observable robustness score and make the new cells a held-out screening exercise.** This is an analysis gap, not necessarily more data. The score should separate at least (i) loss from truthful or prescribed play, (ii) sign stability across model families, and (iii) worst-case degradation under a representation or message perturbation; B's average ranking, sign-mixed clock/menu effects, and E's baseline ceiling make all three necessary. Freeze the score and predictions before examining +X and the confirmatory lie cells. Without a prospective target, the paper repeats S1's stated weakness: it cheaply redescribes known anchors but never demonstrates what a designer learns before paying for a human study.

4. **Run the lie-on-clock crossing if the paper claims that mechanism structure protects implementation.** Cross the true/false/neutral invariant messages with direct revelation versus the OSP clock, holding the underlying incentives as comparable as the design permits. **Assumption:** such a comparison can be constructed without introducing a payoff difference beyond those already inherent in the direct and clock implementations; if not, the paper must narrow the claim. B shows that the OSP clock is the strongest legitimate lever and F suggests that false text can dominate legitimate levers, but only their interaction tests the proposed “gullibility-proofness” idea. Without this cell, the paper may rank structure and text separately; it may not tell designers that structure immunizes participants against manipulative content.

The full PLANNED combined-lever grid is **not** in the minimal set for this story. It becomes necessary only if the paper promises a complete design map or optimal bundles. The proposed story can honestly deliver a ranked single-lever stress test plus the theoretically diagnostic structure-by-lie interaction; claiming substitutes or complements from B's main effects alone would recreate the overreach being removed.
